# NEXTJUDGE Business Strategy

**Decision date:** 2026-11-27  
**Status:** Strategy hypotheses; no revenue or customer traction is claimed.

## 1. Positioning

NEXTJUDGE is a hybrid programming platform with an AI-first learning advantage. It connects problem solving, learning, competition, coding assessment, and education operations.

The primary paid value is not access to a large problem archive. It is time saved, better feedback, measurable learning progress, and reduced operational work for educators and organizations.

## 2. Competitive environment

Baekjoon Online Judge reopened on 2026-11-20 under Day1Company. The official notice states that previous paid services ended and the service is centered on free access. Existing member information, submission records, problems, and judging data were transferred.

Official source: https://www.acmicpc.net/board/view/166446

Response:
- Do not compete on problem volume alone.
- Treat basic online judging as expected baseline functionality.
- Focus on AI-guided learning, skill diagnosis, personalized progression, and educator workflows.
- Build original or properly licensed content; do not scrape or copy competitor content.
- Maintain a free core so adoption is not blocked by a paywall.

## 3. Customer segments and monetization

| Segment | Primary value | Monetization hypothesis | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual learners | AI Coach, skill map, learning plan | Free + capped Pro subscription | P1 |
| Schools and academies | Classroom, assignments, reports, safe AI controls | Per active seat or institution tier | P1 |
| Universities and clubs | Contest operations, team dashboards | Event/organization plan | P2 |
| Employers | Structured coding assessments and reports | Per assessment or contract | P3, after reliability validation |
| Content creators | Original problem/content distribution | Marketplace commission | P3 |

## 4. Differentiation and defensibility

1. **Learning graph:** connect problem concepts, attempts, hints, and independent mastery evidence.
2. **AI feedback loop:** hints and reviews that adapt to demonstrated gaps, with quality evaluation.
3. **Progression:** translate diagnosis into a practical next-step plan.
4. **Education workflow:** teachers assign work and see cohort-level progress without exposing unnecessary student data.
5. **Trust:** reliable judge, original/licensed content, privacy controls, and transparent AI limitations.

These are strategic hypotheses, not proven advantages. Validate through retention, learning outcomes, and paid pilots.

## 5. Go-to-market sequence

### Stage A — Product validation
- Interview individual learners and educators.
- Launch a small, original-problem beta.
- Validate problem solving and AI hint usefulness.
- Measure independent solve rate and repeat learning sessions.

### Stage B — First revenue
- Test capped Pro AI features with clear usage limits.
- Run small academy/classroom pilots.
- Charge only after users demonstrate repeated value.
- Measure AI cost per retained learner.

### Stage C — Repeatable growth
- Self-serve classroom onboarding.
- Teacher-to-student invitation loop.
- Weekly challenges and post-contest review.
- Improve onboarding, support, and reliability.

### Stage D — Enterprise and global
- Expand to hiring assessments only after judge reliability, anti-abuse, privacy, and audit controls are validated.
- Localize product and content, then test regional demand.
- Add regional infrastructure based on measured usage.

## 6. Revenue principles

- Keep basic problem discovery, practice, and core judging accessible.
- Charge for higher-value AI assistance, learning analytics, and institution operations.
- No unlimited AI usage until unit economics are proven.
- Prefer simple, transparent tiers over confusing feature gates.
- Do not use student personal data for advertising.
- Do not claim hiring suitability or learning gains without evidence.

## 7. Key assumptions to validate

- Whether users value progressive hints more than complete solutions.
- Whether AI-assisted learners solve similar problems independently afterward.
- Which AI features create repeat usage.
- Whether schools/academies will pay for workflow and reporting.
- Whether AI costs can fit within a capped subscription.
- Whether a separate paid coding assessment product has sufficient trust and demand.
